## 3. What an auditor must look at first

1. **The payment in `_transferOut` and `_send`** (ETH-7). The claim that a recipient can cost a relayer at most
   `PUSH_GAS` + 2,300 gas and two storage writes; that no return data is copied (assembly `call` with a zero-length
   output, no `returndatacopy` anywhere in the contract); that the gas check
   `gasleft() >= PUSH_GAS * 64 / 63 + 40,000` really guarantees the callee its 30,000 under EIP-150 for every kind
   of callee (cold, empty, with code); and that a failed push leaves the books exact (`claimable`, `totalClaimable`).
2. **Books against balance** (ETH-6). The vault never reads its balance to decide an amount; it only requires
   `balance >= owedWei()`. Check every place ether leaves (`_transferOut`, `_claim`, `sweepFees`) for the order
   "books first, ether after", and that no path pays `balance - something`.
3. **Reentrancy.** Every state-changing external function is `nonReentrant` (the Ethereum vault's guard, unchanged);
   `claimTo`, `claimFor` and `sweepFees` forward all gas. Are there view functions another protocol could read
   mid-payment and be misled by (`owedWei`, `surplusWei`, `claimable`)? The state is consistent at every external
   call: books are updated before the ether moves, and a failed push restores the ether before the claim is booked.
4. **`deposit`** (ETH-4): `msg.value == amount * UNIT` with `amount` a `uint64` (no overflow: `uint256(amount) *
   UNIT`); the backing comparison on `balance - msg.value`; the unchanged recipient rule.
5. **Fee rounding** (ETH-5): `_fee` rounds up; `minDeposit - fee(minDeposit) >= l2MinBurn` in the constructor uses
   the same function; a release with `amount <= fee` is refused in `_checkRelease` (before any state change) and
   again in `_transferOut` (the delayed path).
6. **The window and the cap with deferred payments.** A deferred release charges the bucket; `totalClaimable` is
   outside `totalLocked` and outside the cap (section 4, limit L2).
7. **The launch gate** (`native_ether.py`): two records, fail-closed reading; which tools ask which record; that
   no rehearsal flag reaches a chain-1 config.
8. **The monitors' backing formula** (`reservas.py`, `guardiao.py`): whole units, fees and claims subtracted, never
   above `totalLocked`; forced ether cannot hide a shortfall of backing (it is real ether in the vault; the question
   is whether the formula could count ether twice).

## 4. Known limits (decided, not defects to rediscover)

| # | Limit | Why accepted |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Forced ether is unrecoverable. No function pays out `surplusWei()` | Any function that pays "the balance beyond the books" is a second exit to defend; the amount is somebody's mistake or a donation, never a user's deposit (deposits cannot leave wei behind) |
| L2 | The vault's balance can exceed the cap by unclaimed payments and by forced ether | The cap bounds what the committee can release (`totalLocked`). A waiting payment can only go to its recipient. Counting it against the cap would let one refusing recipient hold deposit room |
| L3 | A burn to a contract that can neither receive ether nor call `claimTo(to)` is never paid | The burn names its recipient; nobody else may redirect it. Pages warn |
| L4 | A guardian pause does not stop `claimTo` / `claimFor`, nor `sweepFees` | A claim is a release already executed; the Ethereum vault does not take back an executed token transfer either |
| L5 | A wallet whose `receive` needs more than 30,000 gas is not paid by the push | `claimFor(recipient)` by anyone, with all gas |
| L6 | Fees are rounded up: a user pays up to one unit (0.000001 ETH) more than the exact rate per operation | Coordinator decision 2: no rounding gain for the user |
| L7 | Six decimals: the smallest amount is 1e12 wei; integrators must read `decimals()` | `docs/BRIDGE-ETH.md` section 3 |
| L8 | The cap and the window are ether amounts: their USD value floats with the price | Stated in the route file with the exposure at twice the price |
| L9 | The minimum burn (7 ETH) makes the route serve large exits only | Follows from "no release relayed at a loss at 30 gwei" with a 10 bps fee; an owner input (`docs/BRIDGE-ETH.md` section 4) |
| L10 | The committee model and everything it implies (a colluding 5 of 7 can release up to the window per day; the mint chain holds exits after a credited forgery; no verifier is live) | The Ethereum bridge's model, unchanged: `docs/AUDIT-SCOPE-BRIDGE-V3.md` sections 4 and 6 |
| L11 | `PUSH_GAS` and the 40,000 reserve are constants of an immutable contract (so is the storage placement of ETH-10, whose only purpose is the relayer's cost). A future fork that made a value-carrying call cost more than 40,000 gas before the callee runs would let a starved push through the check: a payable release could then be booked as a claim (paid later by `claimFor`). No funds would be at risk | The reserve has 3,400 gas of margin over today's worst case (9,000 + 25,000 + 2,600) |
| L12 | The relayer's cost is bounded per release, but a burner may still choose the costliest recipient every time | The minimum burn is derived from that worst case |

## 5. Invariants (asserted by the suites)

- I1 `totalLocked` == sum of net deposits - sum of released amounts.
- I2 `feesAccrued` == fees taken - fees swept; the treasury holds exactly the swept fees.
- I3 `totalClaimable` == deferred payments - claimed payments == sum of `claimable[r]`.
- I4 `balance` == `owedWei()` + forced ether, to the wei; hence `balance >= owedWei()` always.
- I5 `totalLocked <= cap`, whatever was forced in.
- I6 Over any interval of T seconds the released amounts are at most `windowLimit x (1 + T / windowSeconds)`,
  deferred releases included.
- I7 The deposit count never falls; the chain recomputed from genesis over every deposit ends at the vault's head.
- I8 A (nonce, burn) is paid at most once; every recipient holds exactly its certified payouts; nobody else holds any.
- D  On fee-exact amounts and plain recipients, every call has the same outcome, error, books, events and holdings
  on this vault and on the audited Ethereum vault: committee releases, the one-way switch and releases by proof, the
  delayed queue and the forgery latch.
