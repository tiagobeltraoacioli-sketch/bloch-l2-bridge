## 1. Summary for bidders

- **What.** Three components of one launch on the Bloch L2 (chain id 700770, an EVM rollup whose blocks are settled on
  the Bloch L1 by SP1 zero-knowledge proofs):
  1. **Phase 1, the DEX:** a Uniswap-V2-derived AMM with constructor-fixed reserve caps, a k ceiling, a one-shot first
     depositor and an immutable protocol fee recipient. About 1,000 nSLOC of new or changed Solidity logic.
  2. **Phase 2, the bUSDC bridge V3:** an Ethereum USDC vault and a capped, signer-minted L2 token linked by a hash chain
     over deposits, with a one-way switch to an SP1 light-client release proof. Solidity, the Python daemons that hold
     keys or decide, and the SP1 light-client guests.
  3. **Phase 3, the December L2 program swap:** the new SP1 settlement guest with a sparse state witness, L1 forced
     inclusion (guest and L1 queue), the complete-witness fallback guest, and the rule changes that ride with them
     (block derivation, fee-vault sweep, a base-fee floor). Rust. Optionally, a small L1 fee on L2 deposits and claims
     (track 3E).
- **One engagement, three phases, separate line items and separate reports.** We prefer one firm for all three. We
  accept a split: a Solidity team for Phases 1 and 2 track A, and a zkVM/Rust team for Phase 3 and Phase 2 track B.
- **Phase 3 is time-critical.** Its review must start on **2026-11-04** (or within days of it) for the L1 gate of
  2026-12-17 (section 7). Phases 1 and 2 have no fixed date; Phase 1 is frozen now.
- **No proxies and no upgrade paths.** The DEX and bridge contracts are immutable once deployed, and the L2 program is
  pinned in L1 consensus; that is why the audit is before deployment.
- **Known issues are disclosed** (section 6). Please do not spend time rediscovering them; do challenge their severity
  and our mitigations.
- **Wanted:** a written proposal with team, effort in engineer-weeks per phase and track, a fixed price per phase,
  earliest start date per phase, and fix-review terms (section 9).

### 3.2 What differs from Uniswap V2

Full list with file:line references in `docs/AUDIT-PACKAGE-DEX.md` section 3. In short:
- constructor-fixed reserve caps per token, and the **k ceiling** `reserve0 * reserve1 <= 0.64 * cap0 * cap1`
  (`_applyCeiling`, `UniswapV2Pair.sol:203-220`);
- the burn exit bound (DEX-01) and the `EMPTY_POOL` sync rule (DEX-09);
- the one-shot first depositor, checked with `tx.origin` (`UniswapV2Pair.sol:286-295`);
- an immutable `feeTo` (the Bloch Inc Safe) with no setter; 1/6 of the 0.30% fee as LP shares;
- registry `pairFor`, path validation, recipient guards, anchored exact-in swaps;
- an off-chain finality gate that reads everything at the L2 `finalized` block (tier B).

There is no owner, pause, upgrade or proxy anywhere in tier A.

### 3.3 Invariants

I1-I21 of `docs/AUDIT-PACKAGE-DEX.md` section 6, each mapped to a test: k never decreases below the ceiling (I1) and
falls by less than one unit at it (I1b); reserves within caps (I2); the k ceiling (I3); reserves backed (I4); closed LP
accounting (I5); protocol fee equals the v2 closed form (I6); exits never blocked (I7); a swap passes after `skim` (I8);
only documented reverts (I9); constants constant and no admin selector (I10); reentry hits `LOCKED` (I11); rounding
favours the pool (I12); no cap bypass (I13); first-depositor inflation unprofitable (I14); only the first depositor
opens a pair (I15); `skim` returns exactly the excess (I16); Lens parity (I17); anchored swaps (I18); permits (I19);
WBLCH backing (I20); deployment exactness (I21).

### 3.4 Where we most want attention

1. `_applyCeiling`: every branch and rounding direction; whether excess can ever be LP principal.
2. Its interaction with `_mintFee`, `kLast` and burn's exit bound.
3. The first-depositor rule with `tx.origin`, including contracts calling the pair directly.
4. Any sequence that leaves no material swap possible in either direction after a `skim`.
5. Router recipient guards, fee-on-transfer variants with standing balances, native refunds.
6. Lens parity with execution.
7. Tier B: whether a tampered or stale config, plan, opening form or raw-transaction file can deploy other code, or
   seed at another price, fraction or recipient, without a refusal.
8. **Swap fees at the k ceiling** (accepted for launch, section 6.1 item DEX-4): assess the candidate fix "transfer the
   clamped amount to `feeTo` inside `swap`" and propose a better one if it exists. It would be a next-version change.

## 5. Phase 3: the December L2 program (`bloch-l2-evm`, `bloch-sis-pow`)

### 5.1 What the guest does and what a bug costs

The L1 trusts the 766-byte settlement statement the guest commits (previous and new state roots, header hash, deposits
hash, transaction and receipt roots, withdrawal root and count, DA commitment, L1 anchor and slot, program identities).
If the guest accepts a wrong transition, a false withdrawal root is settled and the L1 escrow can be drained. The failure
the audit must rule out is **"the guest commits a statement for a transition that revm would not produce from the
authentic pre-state"**. A bug in forced inclusion has a second failure mode: **settlement halts** (the L1 refuses every
statement) or **a forced transaction is silently dropped**.

### 5.4 Forced inclusion: the rule in one paragraph

A user whom the sequencer censors submits a signed L2 transaction to the L1 in a `0x1C` transaction, paying an L1 fee
and a surcharge priced by queue depth. The L1 queues it. Once the record is "due" at a settlement's L1 anchor, the next
statement must have consumed it: the guest runs every due record right after the deposits, in order, and either
executes it or skips it for one of three stated reasons (does not decode for this chain; its gas limit is above the
declared gas; revm refuses it before execution). The statement's deposits slot becomes
`inbox_hash(deposits_hash, every consumed record)`, so the L1 can check that nothing due was left out. The witness
carries every account the admissibility check reads, and a database failure in that check is an error, never a skip.
Bounds: 16 records and 300,000 declared gas per settlement, 21,000 to 300,000 gas per record, 8 KiB per envelope, 256
weight units in the queue.

**Changes after the internal review (agent 164), in the code under audit.** These were made in round 11 and are new to
every internal review except their own tests:

| Item | Rule now |
|---|---|
| R1 (High) rollback to a program without forced inclusion halts settlement | The arming rule also requires `PROGRAM_FALLBACK` to be listed as implementing forced inclusion, and an unlisted fallback takes no new record at run time. The fallback guest now implements the rule (section 5.2) |
| R2 (Medium) a head reset dropped consumed records | A log of the last 1,024 consumed records; a reset re-queues those above its target at the front; a deeper reset is refused |
| R3 (Medium) drain rate depended on record size | `weight(gas) = ceil(gas * 14 / 300,000)`; each settlement-sized group counts at least 14 units, so a record ending at queue position `p` is consumed by the `ceil(p / 14)`-th settlement anchored after it (19 at capacity), whatever the sizes and mix |
| R4 (Medium) one record ahead invalidated in-flight transactions | The `0x1C` transaction signs `max_surcharge_sat`; the inputs fund it; the surcharge at the depth found is paid to the protocol treasury (output 2) and the rest is refunded to the funding key (output 1); `SurchargeAboveMax` otherwise. The surcharge is no longer added to the base fee. The treasury is a compiled constant (`TBD-owner`); the queue is never enabled without it |
| R5 (Low) whole record priced at its entry tier | Each unit priced at its own position (closed form, at most two tiers) |
| R6 (Low) keeping the queue full was cheap | Price doubles every 24 units instead of 32: the cheapest full hold costs 10,240,000 sat per settlement (10 times the reviewed design), 14,336,000 with transfers |
